# Bridging Theory to Code: Evolving "your-story" Toward the Five-Layer Autonomous AI Game Master Architecture

## TL;DR
- The second-pass research strongly confirms the prior paper's central thesis: newer 2025–2026 academic work (RPGBench, CoC-Seduce/"Seduced by the Narrative," WOLF, SycEval, NARRA-Gym) independently documents that LLMs produce engaging prose but fail at verifiable state tracking, rule adjudication, and resisting persuasive player rhetoric — exactly the failure modes the five-layer engine is designed to defeat. "your-story" in its current single-call form is the naive architecture this literature warns against.
- The three flagged gaps are all solvable on the existing Next.js + Vercel + Supabase + DeepSeek/OpenRouter stack without a rewrite: an event-sourced Postgres state model (append-only log + derived "state cards"), a forced-tool-call dice/oracle layer (DeepSeek strict-mode tool calling with `tool_choice`, plus a text-fallback parser), and a serverless-appropriate memory architecture (rolling summarization + pgvector RAG + canonical-fact cache, all persisted in Postgres because Vercel functions are stateless).
- Recommended sequence: (1) externalize state to Postgres and inject it as structured context; (2) add the oracle/validation layer via forced tool calls; (3) add rolling-summary + pgvector memory; (4) adopt the Vercel AI SDK's `generateObject`/`toolChoice` to make model-swapping and structured output robust. Prioritize the oracle+validation layer — it delivers the largest correctness gain per unit of effort.

## Key Findings

### Theory extension
- **RPGBench (arXiv:2502.00595)** is the most direct new corroboration. Authored by an 11-person team led by Pengfei Yu (Boson AI) and accepted to NeurIPS 2025, it benchmarks LLMs as RPG *engines* across two tasks, Game Creation and Game Simulation, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00595) with the simulation loop broken into three per-round stages (Event Planning, Game Narration, Game State Updates). Its headline finding, verbatim: "state-of-the-art LLMs can produce engaging stories but often struggle to implement consistent, verifiable game mechanics, particularly in long or complex scenarios." [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00595) This is empirical support for separating narration (LLM) from state/rules (deterministic engine).
- **"Seduced by the Narrative" / CoC-Seduce (arXiv:2607.02802)** is now verified as a real preprint (Chen et al., University of Alberta + Qilu University of Technology + "N-Dice Association"). It introduces "Rhetorical Injection" attacks and a 5,376-sample benchmark on *Call of Cthulhu* adjudication (1,792 scenarios per source model, 16 skill categories, 4 world settings). [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02802) Across 20 target adjudicator models, "neither model scale nor explicit reasoning mechanisms reliably confer adjudication robustness," with PSEUDO-LOGIC the dominant attack vector and cross-cultural settings exposing systematic knowledge gaps. [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02802) It formalizes the GM-as-adjudicator as a binary validity function V(C, Sₜ) ∈ {roll required, automatic success}. **Caveat: the paper is forward-dated (references GPT-5.4, Claude Sonnet 4.6, Gemini 3.5 Flash, and "OpenAI 2026") and is a very recent, non-peer-reviewed preprint; treat its specific numbers cautiously, but its thesis is corroborated by the independent sycophancy literature below.**
- **Sycophancy is now heavily corroborated** beyond the original Anthropic paper (Sharma et al., arXiv:2310.13548): SycEval (arXiv:2502.08177) shows even frontier models "frequently soften or change responses under user disagreement"; [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.10061) SYCON-Bench (EMNLP 2025 Findings) measures "Turn of Flip" and "Number of Flips" under sustained user pressure; [GitHub](https://github.com/JiseungHong/SYCON-Bench) and multiple 2025–2026 papers (e.g., arXiv:2508.02087 on the internal origins of sycophancy) tie the behavior to RLHF. This is the mechanistic root of the "auto-success" and "illusionism collapse" failure modes.
- **Generative Agents (Park et al., arXiv:2304.03442, UIST 2023)** is the key memory-architecture precedent, and its retrieval mechanism is precise enough to copy directly: memories are scored by **recency** (exponential decay since last access), **importance** (an LLM-assigned 1–10 score at creation time), and **relevance** (embedding cosine similarity); [Subodh Jena](https://www.subodhjena.com/blog/generative-agents-memory-stanford) the three are normalized and combined with equal weighting, [Medium](https://artgor.medium.com/paper-review-generative-agents-interactive-simulacra-of-human-behavior-cc5f8294b4ac) and reflection fires when cumulative importance crosses ~150 points. Secondary analyses call this design "the reference design for persistent agent memory." [Subodh Jena](https://www.subodhjena.com/blog/generative-agents-memory-stanford) It directly informs layer 4.
- **Drama-management lineage** is richer than Façade alone: Weyhrauch's *Guiding Interactive Drama* (CMU PhD, 1997), the Oz Project, Riedl & Young narrative mediation/IPOCL, Nelson/Mateas/Roberts search-based drama management (MDP trajectory targeting), Mott & Lester's U-DIRECTOR (decision-theoretic narrative planning), Sharma/Ontañón/Mehta/Ram's case-based C-DraGer (which produced a measured ~12.5% subjective improvement in play experience), and Ware et al.'s "Multi-Agent Narrative Experience Management as Story Graph Pruning" (AIIDE 2019). The essential modern survey is **Kreminski & Wardrip-Fruin, "Generatively Authored Narrative Experiences: A Survey of Recent Advances" (ACM Computing Surveys 55(4), 2023)**. There is also emerging LLM-native drama-management work: a "Fabula" storytelling sidekick (arXiv:2606.14411) that delegates planning to a "drama manager" invoking LLMs with structured outputs across three abstraction levels (story plan, beat plan, script), [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.14411) and an "Authoring Framework for LLM-Based Drama Managers" listed in Kreminski's group's output.
- **Adjacent hidden-state multi-agent research** (Werewolf/social deduction) reinforces the point: WOLF (arXiv:2512.09187, NeurIPS 2025 workshop) shows LLMs "deceive convincingly but remain weak at detecting deception" (werewolves lie in 31% of turns; peer detection only ~52% accurate), and these systems only work when wrapped in a "programmable LangGraph state machine with strict night–day cycles" [OpenReview](https://openreview.net/forum?id=ERw9r7wPzl) — i.e., an external deterministic authority, exactly the paper's layer 2/5. Triadic Werewolf (arXiv:2606.27909) and Bayesian Social Deduction with Graph-Informed Language Models (arXiv:2506.17788) extend this.
- **New citable datasets/benchmarks beyond CALYPSO/RPGBench:**
  - *"Dungeons and Dragons as a Dialog Challenge for Artificial Intelligence"* (Callison-Burch, Tomar, Martin, Ippolito, Bailis & Reitter; EMNLP 2022; arXiv:2210.07109). Dataset: nearly 900 games, 7,000 players, 800,000 dialogue turns, 500,000 dice rolls, 58M words. [arXiv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.07109) Critically for the state-tracking argument, its best game-state tracker (DND-GST) hit only **58% accuracy**, and the authors note this "likely falls short of being useful" [University of Pennsylvania](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~ccb/publications/dungeons-and-dragons-as-a-dialog-challenge-for-artificial-intelligence.pdf) — hard evidence that LLMs alone cannot reliably track game state.
  - *FIREBALL* (Zhu, Aggarwal, Feng, Martin, Callison-Burch) — a D&D actual-play dataset with *structured* game-state information, showing LLMs with access to state generate higher-quality turns than history-only LLMs.
  - *NARRA-Gym* (arXiv:2605.08503) — benchmarks interactive narrative agents on StoryQ/UX rubrics; Claude Sonnet 4.6 ranked highest, with DeepSeek V4, GPT-5.4, and DeepSeek V3.2 clustered slightly behind.
  - A 2025 Utrecht University master's thesis (Sakellaridis) built an LLM DM trained on *Critical Role* transcripts; it scored 4.13/5 on immersion vs 3.35 for human DMs but concluded LLMs should augment, not replace, human DMs.

### Gap 1: Deterministic state/world model on Postgres/Supabase
- **Event sourcing is the strongest fit** for a story engine: an append-only `events` table is the single source of truth; current state is a *projection* derived by replay, with periodic snapshots (every 100–500 events) for performance. This gives a free audit trail, time-travel ("what did the world look like at scene 12"), clean save states, and the ability to add new read models retroactively — all things a narrative engine wants. The write must happen in the same DB transaction as any state mutation. Tradeoffs: added operational complexity and eventual consistency of read models. [Letsbuildsolutions](https://letsbuildsolutions.com/blog/system-design/event-sourcing-in-practice-building-an-append-only-event-store-with-projections-and-snapshots/) Reference implementations exist for Postgres (eugene-khyst/postgresql-event-sourcing; Marten for .NET).
- **JSONB vs normalized:** the consensus is hybrid. Use normalized columns for entities you query/filter/join on (characters, threads, flags, relationships) and JSONB for fuzzy, evolving, whole-object payloads (event bodies, lore blobs). The warnings are concrete and quantified: PostgreSQL keeps **no statistics inside JSONB** and falls back to a hardcoded 0.1% selectivity estimate, [Pulse](https://pulse.support/kb/postgresql-json-vs-jsonb) so filtering/joining on JSONB fields can be catastrophically slow — Heap's engineering team measured **"584 seconds… about 2000x slower"** [Heap](https://www.heap.io/blog/when-to-avoid-jsonb-in-a-postgresql-schema) than the normalized equivalent, and the JSONB table used **164 MB vs 79 MB** on disk ("more than twice as much"). [Rssing](https://hemming-in.rssing.com/chan-2212310/all_p206.html) JSONB documents over 2KB also get TOASTed (extra I/O), and partial in-place updates of large JSONB are awkward.
- **State-as-context injection:** feed the LLM a compact, rendered "state card" (a structured system-prompt block containing only the currently-relevant entities/flags), not the raw DB. This is the pattern AI Dungeon converged on (Plot Essentials, Story Cards, Author's Note) and LoreKeeper's "Codex." It keeps grounding tight without blowing the context budget.

### Gap 2: External entropy / oracle layer
- **Forced tool-calling is the mechanism.** OpenAI supports `tool_choice: "required"` (must call some tool) and forced-function `tool_choice: {"type":"function","name":"roll_dice"}` (in Chat Completions the nested form is `{"type":"function","function":{"name":"..."}}`). Verbatim from OpenAI's function-calling docs: "**Required:** Call one or more functions. `tool_choice: "required"`… **Forced Function:** Call exactly one specific function." [OpenAI Developers](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/function-calling) The Vercel AI SDK exposes the same: verbatim, "`required`: the model must call a tool," [AI SDK](https://ai-sdk.dev/docs/ai-sdk-core/tools-and-tool-calling) and force-a-specific-tool via `toolChoice: { type: 'tool', toolName: 'rollDice' }`.
- **DeepSeek supports OpenAI-style tool calling and a Beta strict JSON-schema mode.** Verbatim from DeepSeek's tool-calls guide: "In `strict` mode, the model strictly adheres to the format requirements of the Function's JSON schema when outputting a tool call… supported by both thinking and non-thinking mode" [deepseek](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/guides/tool_calls/) — enabled via `base_url=".../beta"`, `strict: true` on each function, with server-side schema validation (objects must set all properties `required` and `additionalProperties: false`; `minLength`/`maxItems` are unsupported). **Reliability caveat:** the official DeepSeek repo issue #1244 (corroborated by sglang, vllm, and ollama issues) documents that DeepSeek can intermittently "emit tool calls as plain text in content instead of tool_calls," [GitHub](https://github.com/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V3/issues/1244) with `finish_reason: stop` and a null `tool_calls` field. Any oracle layer must defensively parse `content` for a tool-call-shaped payload and re-dispatch. Also note: `tool_choice: "required"` is not enumerated verbatim on DeepSeek's guide page (only OpenAI-compatibility is claimed) — verify against the live API reference.
- **Oracle-as-a-tool open-source prior art exists:** an "RPG Tools" MCP server exposing `roll_dice`, `check_success`, and `generate_event` (Mythic-GME-style random events); [MCP Market](https://mcpmarket.com/server/rpg-tools) `serelon/rpg-tools`, a stdlib-only solo-RPG oracle that "provides raw symbolic material—[the LLM] interprets it creatively in context" [GitHub](https://github.com/serelon/rpg-tools) with a yes/no fate oracle across likelihood tiers; and `OptionalRule/ttrpg-dice-claude-skill`, which uses a cryptographically-secure RNG and returns explicit provenance (`"rng": {"source": "CSPRNG"}`). These validate the "engine rolls, LLM narrates" contract.
- Server-side RNG in a Vercel function is trivial and genuinely LLM-independent; the design point is *architectural* — the roll happens outside the model and the result is injected as ground truth the model must narrate around — which directly defeats sycophancy/auto-success.

### Gap 3: Memory/context architecture for serverless
- **Vercel functions are stateless per-invocation** (read-only filesystem except a 500 MB `/tmp` scratch that does not persist, no durable RAM, DB connections not shared across cold boots). Fluid Compute (default since early 2025) adds in-instance concurrency and V8 bytecode caching and "scale to one" warm instances — Vercel claims cold starts are eliminated for 99.37% of requests on sustained traffic — but it does **not** provide durable cross-request agent memory. Therefore all memory must live in Postgres/Supabase, not in the function. This is the key structural difference from a persistent-agent architecture: every turn reconstructs its working memory from the database.
- **The proven pattern is three-tier memory:** (1) short-term = recent turns kept verbatim; (2) rolling summarization = older turns compressed into a running summary (AI Dungeon's "Auto Summary," which it built using "summarization models, embeddings, vectors, and embedding models"); (3) long-term = pgvector semantic retrieval over story history + lore entries, plus a canonical-fact table (structured SQL) for exact facts. Supabase natively supports all of this (pgvector + a `match_documents()` similarity function). Supabase's own "natural-db" demo combines message history (short-term), pgvector semantic memory, and structured SQL memory — a direct template.
- **Karpathy's "LLM wiki" pattern** (pre-compiling sources into a persistent, interlinked wiki of summaries/entity pages, synthesized once so knowledge compounds instead of being re-derived per query) is being applied to exactly this problem by **LoreKeeper**, an AI-GM virtual tabletop that stores every NPC/place/pact in a "Codex" (embedded and stored with pgvector in Postgres) and has the GM read it each turn. Its blog frames the insight precisely: "The context window is not memory. It's a whiteboard that gets wiped after every session." [LoreKeeper](https://lore-keeper.com/blog/llm-wiki-for-rpg) This is a concrete production template for "your-story."

### DeepSeek model characteristics for a GM role
- Context window ~128K tokens (V3/V3.2 families; V4 line reportedly ~1M), output typically capped ~4K–8K tokens, and no durable cross-chat memory (you supply it). DeepSeek-V3.2 exhibits "redundant self-verification" that can blow past the 128K limit in long agentic loops — relevant if you chain many tool calls per turn.
- On creative writing, independent comparisons place DeepSeek behind Claude and the GPT-5.x line on raw prose quality but competitive on cost and strong on analytical breadth. On price, published rate cards put DeepSeek-V3 at ~$0.27/1M input and ~$0.40/1M output vs GPT-4o's $5.00/$15.00 — roughly **18× cheaper on input and ~37× on output**; the newer V4-Flash is quoted at $0.14/$0.28 per 1M tokens, "roughly 35 to 100 times cheaper than GPT-5.5 or Claude Opus 4.8" [Tech Jacks Solutions](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-tools/deepseek/deepseek-pricing/) (vendor/blog figures — treat as indicative). NARRA-Gym ranked Claude Sonnet 4.6 highest on story-quality/UX, with DeepSeek and GPT-5.4 clustered slightly behind. **Implication:** the project's existing OpenRouter model-swapping is a genuine asset — use DeepSeek as the cost-efficient default for narration, route adjudication/validation to a strong instruction-follower or pure code, and keep the option to escalate premium narration to Claude.

### Stack-specific architecture
- **Vercel AI SDK is the recommended orchestration layer.** `generateObject`/`streamObject` (Zod-schema-validated structured output with automatic retry on invalid JSON — "near-zero JSON parsing errors" reported vs prompt-based extraction) should replace "your-story's" current fragile plain-text-fallback JSON wrapper. A provider registry lets you swap DeepSeek/OpenRouter/Claude by changing one argument; `useObject`/`streamText` handle streaming. This directly hardens the strict `{content, imageUrl, user}` contract.
- **Streaming vs structured tradeoff:** stream narration text for UX, but generate state mutations and tool calls as validated structured objects. `streamObject` can stream partial objects to avoid layout shift while still validating against schema.
- **Supabase Edge Functions vs Next.js API routes:** keep orchestration in the Next.js API route (co-located, already there); use Supabase for auth, Postgres, pgvector, and pg_cron/pg_net if you later want scheduled "offscreen world" updates (fronts/clocks advancing between sessions).
- **Token economy:** the credit/token model "your-story" already has is the industry-standard hybrid for AI SaaS (subscription + prepaid credits/generations, with token-level cost telemetry hidden behind a user-facing "generation" unit — "The user sees '8 credits'; finance and engineering see token-level cost"). [QubitTool](https://qubittool.com/blog/ai-saas-global-pricing-token-subscription) Documented pitfalls to heed: credits are a *deferred-revenue liability* until consumed (ASC 606 + breakage on expiry); hidden multi-call cost (RAG + summarization + validation + generation) routinely adds **40–60%** on top of the visible generation call; and "bill shock" from runaway loops. Bound tool-call loops with explicit step limits — this also mitigates the Vercel AI SDK `toolChoice:'required'` infinite-loop bug (vercel/ai issue #3944).

## Details

### Why the current architecture is the "naive" one, precisely
"your-story" currently does whole-state → one model call → next `ScenePart`. Every failure mode in the literature flows from having no authority outside the model:
- **State/memory drift:** confirmed universal in AI Dungeon ("as the story grows, the system pushes older details out of context… eventually your character cards are ignored") and quantified academically (D&D dialog-challenge state tracking at only 58% accuracy, which the authors themselves call short of useful).
- **Sycophancy / auto-success / illusionism collapse:** the model, optimized to be agreeable, treats a persuasive player statement as a creative prompt to accommodate rather than a claim to adjudicate (CoC-Seduce's core finding — "they instinctively treat player inputs as creative writing prompts to be accommodated rather than structural game states to be rigorously adjudicated"; [arxiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.02802) SycEval/SYCON-Bench corroboration).
- **Rule hallucination & pacing collapse:** RPGBench shows mechanics break down "in long or complex scenarios."

The fix is the "LLM proposes, deterministic engine disposes" contract: the model may *propose* state changes and narration, but a deterministic Postgres-backed engine + oracle + validator decide what becomes canon.

### Concrete migration blueprint for the five layers on this stack
1. **Game-State/World Model (Postgres, event-sourced):** `events` (append-only; JSONB body + typed columns for `entity_id`, `event_type`, `scene_id`, `seq`), plus projected read tables `entities`, `threads`, `flags`, `relationships`. Snapshot every N events. Save States become "replay to seq N." Client-side E2E encryption complicates server-side projections — see Caveats.
2. **Oracle/Authority (forced tool call):** expose `roll_oracle(question, odds)` and `roll_dice(notation)` as server-side tools; on adjudication turns set `tool_choice` to force them; RNG runs in the Vercel function; the result is injected as an immutable fact. Parse `content` defensively for DeepSeek's text-fallback bug.
3. **Director/Pacing:** store a Chaos-Factor scalar, clocks, and fronts as rows; a deterministic function advances them; the LLM is told the current pacing state in the state card and instructed (not trusted) to honor it.
4. **Memory:** recent turns verbatim + rolling summary row per chapter + pgvector table over scenes and lore entries + canonical-fact table. Retrieve top-k relevant memories per turn using the Generative Agents recency/importance/relevance scoring.
5. **Adjudication/Validation/Guardrail:** after the LLM proposes structured mutations (via `generateObject`), a deterministic validator checks them against current state (does the NPC exist? is the item in inventory? did the roll succeed?) before committing to the event log. Route this check to a strong instruction-following model or, better, pure code.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Externalize state and harden output (highest ROI, do first).**
- Move authoritative state out of the single `storyData` blob into an event-sourced Postgres schema; render a compact "state card" into the system prompt instead of dumping everything.
- Replace the plain-text JSON fallback with Vercel AI SDK `generateObject` + a Zod schema for `{content, imageUrl, user, proposedMutations[]}`.
- *Benchmark to advance:* state no longer drifts across ≥20 scenes in manual playtest; JSON parse failures → ~0.

**Stage 2 — Add the oracle + validation layer (largest correctness gain).**
- Implement server-side `roll_dice`/`roll_oracle` tools and force them on adjudication turns via `tool_choice`; add a deterministic validator that rejects/repairs mutations contradicting state before they hit the event log.
- Add defensive parsing for DeepSeek's content-field tool-call fallback; bound any tool loop with explicit max-steps.
- *Benchmark:* run a mini CoC-Seduce-style test — feed persuasive "I automatically succeed" inputs and confirm the engine still forces a roll. If false-pass rate is non-trivial, keep adjudication in code, not the model.

**Stage 3 — Memory architecture.**
- Add rolling per-chapter summaries + pgvector retrieval over scenes/lore + a canonical-fact table. Model it on Supabase's three-tier "natural-db" and LoreKeeper's Codex.
- *Benchmark:* the model correctly recalls an entity introduced 50+ scenes earlier without it being in the recent window.

**Stage 4 — Director/pacing + model routing.**
- Add Chaos-Factor/clocks/fronts as state rows advanced by deterministic code; use the OpenRouter swap to route generation (DeepSeek, cheap) vs adjudication/premium narration (Claude/GPT-class).
- *Threshold to switch default model:* if playtest StoryQ or rule-adherence on your own eval drops, escalate the narration model; if cost per session exceeds your credit economics, keep DeepSeek and shorten context via better summarization.

**Cross-cutting product/cost guardrails.**
- Track token-level cost per generation internally even though users see "generations"; account for credits as deferred revenue with a breakage policy; add hard per-session spend/loop caps to prevent bill shock; expect true per-generation cost to run 40–60% above the visible model call once RAG/summarization/validation are added.

## Caveats
- **Forward-dated / non-peer-reviewed sources:** CoC-Seduce (arXiv:2607.02802) and several model comparisons reference 2026 models (GPT-5.x, Claude Opus 4.x, DeepSeek V4) and read as very recent preprints or vendor/blog content; their thesis is well-corroborated but specific numbers should not be treated as settled. Model-comparison pieces (Tom's Guide, Substack, tech-insider.org, techjacksolutions.com pricing) are non-authoritative and commercially motivated.
- **GitHub repo internals not accessible:** as in the prior pass, "your-story's" actual prompt-construction code and `docs/` folder could not be read (robots.txt). All statements about its current internals are inferred from the task description, not verified against source.
- **Vendor incentives:** Vercel, Supabase, DeepSeek, and AI-billing vendors describe their own products favorably; the technical claims (serverless statelessness, pgvector, Fluid Compute behavior, tool-calling parameters) are consistent across independent sources, but the "use our product" framing should be discounted. Issue-tracker bug reports (DeepSeek #1244, vercel/ai #3944) are authoritative for the *existence* of the bugs, not for intended behavior.
- **Client-side E2E encryption tension (important):** "your-story's" zero-knowledge AES-256-GCM design means the server cannot read story content — which conflicts with server-side state projection, pgvector embedding, and summarization (all of which need plaintext). Resolving this likely requires doing state/memory derivation *client-side*, or consciously relaxing zero-knowledge for the state/oracle/memory layers specifically. This is a genuine architectural conflict the user must decide on; it was not fully resolvable from available information and should be treated as the single biggest open design question for this migration.
- **DeepSeek `tool_choice: "required"` not verbatim-documented** on DeepSeek's tool-calls guide page (only OpenAI-compatibility is claimed); verify against the live API reference before depending on forced tool calls with DeepSeek specifically, and keep the text-fallback parser regardless.
