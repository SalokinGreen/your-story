# How tabletop adventures and campaigns are designed and run — research for the campaign redesign (2026-10-25)

A research pass (Claude Haiku researcher) for the author's ask to look "at how
the best TTRPG adventures are designed and used", grounded in real play first
(CLAUDE.md, "Real play is the base, fiction is the sky"). **Sourcing caveats
from the researcher:** several primary pages were unreachable (the Blades
clocks page, the Ironsworn SRD, Bryce Lynch's review-standards page, Ron
Edwards's original lines-and-veils text); those points rest on secondary
sources. Two claims in the brief did not check out: Stonetop is a PbtA
"hearth fantasy" game, not a hexcrawl with myths (that is Mythic Bastionland),
and no primary source was found for an OSR "procedures and situations" framing.

## 1. How GMs prep

- **The Lazy DM** (Mike Shea, *Return of the Lazy Dungeon Master*): eight
  modular steps:
  1. review the characters (goals, connections, a hook);
  2. a **strong start**;
  3. a few potential scenes, discarded freely;
  4. **secrets and clues** written without deciding where they are found;
  5. locations described only as far as they matter;
  6. important NPCs;
  7. monsters grouped by location;
  8. treasure.

  Unrevealed secrets carry forward from session to session. About 30 minutes
  of prep per session. ([eight steps](https://slyflourish.com/eight_steps_2023.html))
- **The Alexandrian** (Justin Alexander):
  - **The three clue rule:** for any conclusion you want the players to reach,
    include at least three clues ([?p=7985](https://thealexandrian.net/?p=7985)).
  - **Don't prep plots:** a plot is a fixed chain of events, a situation is
    circumstances whose outcome depends on the players. Prep what the
    opposition wants and does if the players stay out
    ([dont-prep-plots](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/4147/roleplaying-games/dont-prep-plots)).
  - **Tools, not contingencies**
    ([link](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/37422/roleplaying-games/dont-prep-plots-tools-not-contingencies)).
  - **Node-based scenario design:** scenarios linked by clues
    ([series](https://thealexandrian.net/?p=45268)).
  - **Trinity toolboxes:** conflicts, NPCs, locations, atmosphere, rumours and
    themes, three of each
    ([smart prep](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/tag/smart-prep)).
  - Collected in *So You Want to Be a Game Master* (2024).

## 2. Campaign frameworks

- **Dungeon World fronts:**
  - A front has 2–3 **dangers**, each with an impulse and an impending doom
    (Tyranny, Pestilence, Destruction, Usurpation, Impoverishment, Rampant
    Chaos).
  - **Grim portents:** 1–3 per adventure front, 3–5 per campaign front.
  - 1–3 **stakes questions**, and a cast.
  - The GM pushes a danger to its next portent when play reveals it, or as a
    hard move after a failed roll. When all its portents have happened, the
    doom sets in. Stakes are answered by play, not scripted.
  - A front moves whether or not the players engage.
  - Sources: [roll20 compendium](https://roll20.net/compendium/dw/Fronts),
    [Sly Flourish](https://slyflourish.com/fronts_in_dnd.html).
  - GM principles include "ask questions and use the answers" and "be a fan of
    the characters".
- **Blades in the Dark:** progress clocks track effort or looming trouble and
  fill from actions, consequences and fortune rolls; faction clocks hold each
  faction's long-term goal, advanced in downtime
  ([roll20](https://roll20.net/compendium/BITD/Rules:Progress%20clocks), secondary).
- **Ironsworn / Starforged:** ten-box progress tracks, ranks from Troublesome
  to Epic, progress rolls, vows that drive advancement, oracles as the solo
  prompt engine ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironsworn)). This
  is the engine Blood & Bone builds on.
- **Mythic GM Emulator:**
  - a chaos factor of 1–9, lowered when a scene goes to plan and raised when
    it spirals;
  - a d10 against chaos at each scene start: normal, altered or interrupted;
  - weighted threads and characters lists;
  - the fate chart.

  Secondary sources. Already in the app (`mythic.ts`).
- **Mythic Bastionland:** a hexcrawl whose **Myths** are self-contained forces
  that advance as players explore. Each omen is a small scene of that
  progress, and the players find a myth through clues
  ([review](https://familiarwaves.substack.com/p/mythic-bastionland)).
- **West Marches:** a rotating pool of players, no plot, and a frontier that
  goes only one way
  ([guide](https://www.westmarches.games/guide/what-is-west-marches)). A
  sandbox still needs hooks and a default action
  ([Alexandrian](https://thealexandrian.net/wordpress/45321/roleplaying-games/thought-of-the-day-sandbox-simulation)).
- **The five-room dungeon:** guardian, puzzle or roleplay, setback, climax,
  reward or twist. Criticised as linear, and fixed with 2–3 options per stage
  ([Gnome Stew](https://gnomestew.com/the-nine-forms-of-the-five-room-dungeon/)).
- **Adventure paths vs sandbox vs episodic:** an adventure path is chapters in
  a fixed order with choices inside them; a sandbox is hard to prep far ahead.
  Forum-level sources only.

## 3. What makes an adventure usable at the table

- **Bryce Lynch** (tenfootpole), from his reviews:
  - detail alone is "almost always useless";
  - if something is not there, don't note it;
  - keep read-aloud short;
  - keyed entries should scan fast;
  - show what is unusual and interactive.

  ([tenfootpole](https://tenfootpole.org/); the formal criteria page was
  unreachable.)
- **Adventure jams** score "easy to use on the fly". That is opinion, not a
  standard.
- **NPC wants, secrets and voice:** no source in this pass covers them. That
  is a gap.

## 4. Session zero

- **Safety tools:**
  - the **X-card** (John Stavropoulos);
  - **lines and veils** (Ron Edwards): lines never appear, veils happen off
    screen. Revisited during play.

  Sources: [Roll20](https://blog.roll20.net/posts/guest-blog-an-introduction-to-lines-and-veils/),
  [Daggerheart](https://www.daggerheart.com/sessionzero/), secondary.
- **Collaborative world building:**
  - **Microscope** (Ben Robbins, 2011, a GM-less timeline game;
    [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microscope_(role-playing_game)));
  - **Dawn of Worlds** (players as deities building a map; designer
    unconfirmed);
  - the Same Page Tool and Fate's Phase Trio were not verified.

## 5. Pacing

- "Yes, and" comes from improv, and its use in RPGs is contested.
- "Yes, but" / "No, but" are from a D&D Beyond column.
- Cliffhangers, spotlight and in medias res were not researched. That is a
  gap.

## 6. A menu of campaign parts

| Part | From | What the GM does with it in play |
|---|---|---|
| **Pitch and tone, with lines and veils** | Session zero tools | Checks every scene against it; keeps veils off screen |
| **Starting situation** (a strong start, or a proper arrival) | Lazy DM; Alexandrian; decision 197 | Opens on it, with a hook for each character |
| **Factions with goals and clocks** | Blades | Advances each clock in downtime or on a failed action |
| **Fronts: dangers, impulses, grim portents, doom, stakes questions** | Dungeon World | Pushes a danger to its next portent when play reveals it or after a miss |
| **Locations, keyed, a few features each** | Lazy DM; Lynch | Enters them when the players go there |
| **NPC roster with wants, secrets and voice** | Lazy DM (the wants and voice part is a source gap) | Knows what each wants when the players are not there |
| **Secrets and clues, three per conclusion** | Lazy DM; Alexandrian | Places a clue in any scene that suits it |
| **Situations, not a fixed plot** | Alexandrian | Runs each situation's opposition as it stands |
| **Random tables** (oracles, names, loot) | Mythic; Ironsworn; Lazy DM | Rolls to fill gaps and answer questions |
| **Monsters by location** | Lazy DM | Improvises encounters |
| **Threads and clocks** | Mythic; Blades | Each advances on its own schedule |
| **Character ties and session-one hooks** | Lazy DM; Dungeon World | Each scene leads to a character's connection |
| **End states** | DW stakes questions; Alexandrian | The possible conclusions, reached by play |

**Which parts suit which style:**

| Style | Parts |
|---|---|
| Episodic / one-shot | Strong start, locations, monsters, one front, shallow clues |
| Sandbox / West Marches | Locations, factions with clocks, situations, tables, a default action; few beats |
| Adventure path | Ordered beats, a clue network per chapter, set end states; least flexible |
| Solo / oracle-driven | Oracles and chaos, threads and characters lists, vows or goals as the engine |
| Mystery | A three-clue network, timed secrets, NPC secrets, fronts with stakes questions |
| Horror | Fronts with grim portents and dooms, veils, slow clocks |
| Slice of life | NPC wants and relationships, locations, small clocks, few dooms |

## 7. What these facts mean for the app

- **Clocks and portents work only if something advances them.** In the app
  `auto_advance` timers never tick (§6.1), so a clock needs an engine tick or
  a tool call.
- **Every pinned note costs prompt.** The GM carries pinned notes in full, up
  to a shared cap, so wants and clues belong in notes read on demand, not in
  one pinned note.
- **The three clue rule is a prep rule.** The engine could count clues per
  conclusion when the campaign is written; it cannot check what the players
  discovered.
- **Tables and oracles drive sandbox and solo play.** They should roll through
  the engine's seeded dice.
- **Situations over scripted beats (opinion).** "Situations, not plots" fits an
  LLM GM better than scripted beats, because the GM can reason from the
  situation's state. The sources support this for human GMs; there is no LLM
  evidence.
